**Disclaimer:** *This experiment is based on a personal anecdote, and is not intended to be scientific. But it was interesting nonetheless!*

On Sep 18, 2025 12:00 PM GMT+2 I withdrew myself from using most forms of Conversational Generative AI as a helping hand in my work and life. This includes:

1. ChatGPT
2. Google Gemini
3. Perplexity
4. DeepSeek
5. Claude
6. Cohere
7. Character.AI
8. And all the other chatbot websites.

I deleted all my chat history with these websites (except Character.AI where I'm not able to see a 'Delete Chat' option for some reason)

The only exceptions are:

1. Google NotebookLM (which is too much of a productivity booster to ignore, but not really a conversational agent),
2. AI Agents (which are part of my work and studies themselves),
3. Nano Banana (for the giggles, and some editing).
4. Grammarly (the OG text proofreader — even there, I just used the normal tools without relying on the AI agents of Grammarly).

The questions I want to explore are:

1. How long can I sustain myself without the use of the above banned forms of GenAI?
2. Will I retain, boost, or lose out, on my current productivity level?
3. Am I going to develop a new form of personal wisdom, which has previously been unobserved in myself?
4. Am I going to uncover new insights about mindful use of Generative AI, which will take me back to using Generative AI but with a fresh perspective?

The first few hours — was incredibly painful. Looking at my assignments and studies made my head spin and my brain go inactive. I successfully completed an assignment following the above rules, and I started crying (okay, maybe not literally). For the first time ever, I felt a sense of loneliness. And then I realized, I have been using LLMs as too much of a utility that withdrawing from it basically looks like a void in my brain.

1. It has been a companion to me, so without it I feel lonely.
2. It has been a teaching assistant to me, so without it I feel dumb.

And trust me, feeling lonely + dumb together is very bad.

But soon after, the changes started coming up — both in terms of improved actions and new insights. I started using Google Search meticulously. I even avoided the AI responses when searching google. This made me not just take the first answer that comes directly, but search for the most reliable source of the answer as well. And I made sure that I actually searched for the questions only which I did not know the answer to. This brought up an important insight — We tend to use Generative AI as confirmation bias. The problem with most generative AI is that we can prompt it in a way that steers the conversation toward our pre-existing beliefs, and it will often validate our perspective without challenging us much. I discovered that I've almost always used Generative AI as a walking stick, i.e., a helping hand for every small boilerplate task, but not really for knowledge enhancement. The realization is that AI can serve as a knowledge provider (RAG, LLM model training on large data) and knowledge organizer, and not a knowledge creator or knowledge verifier.

The next observation is that I gradually started to execute my tasks with an enhanced sense of precision. This led me to the insight that I was really unaware of differentiating the Good AI responses from the Bad AI responses. The rule of thumb is — When AI generates information based on the sources you *know* are good, it is generally a good AI response. Random scientific, medical, technological and engineering solutions may be presented with a ton of misinformation because we are not even aware what it is trained on. If it had been trained on a vast variety of contradictory sources for example, the same AI platform can give different answers at different times. What AI is rather good at, is reading comprehension. Give it a single, high quality PDF, and now the probability of giving made-up facts becomes much less. In short, know your sources. What was especially fascinating to me is that I was consciously thinking about and applying the first level of insights within just 12 hours of withdrawing from Conversational Generative AI.

Later, this sounded to me like I'm suggesting not to use Generative AI at all, unless we're using it like RAG (i.e., just for your sources). But I got a small mental clarification — AI never gives out 100% correct results. But a lot of the time, whether it is work, academia, or doing some high stakes tasks, the demand would be 100%. So if you're doing a task which demands 100%, do not rely on AI. If you have absolutely ZERO IDEA on your task and want some sort of a starting point, you can use AI, but remember — ANY solution that AI gives out, is ONLY a starting point and has to go through TONS of human-in-the loop verification and polishing. Doing irreversible tasks with AI is almost never a good idea, unless it works on highly known and verified sources and is carefully steered through using ultra-specific prompts and special MCP servers which convert random prompts to deterministic functionality (which sometimes can become counterproductive when using AI). If you want to go a step above irreversibility, suppose there is a task which starts out with a draft and needs ton of polishing ANYWAY, then using AI is already a bad idea in the first place, cause it's almost ALWAYS gonna be more productive to your write your own drafts.

Then slowly the productivity lows started happening. My experiment cost me several extra hours per assignment completion and study activity. This shows that the modern-age productivity standard is extremely high, thanks to AI, and that's a good thing, provided we follow the above insights. I spent an entire day on an assignment where I could have spent just 3 hours to complete it. Another interesting yet dreadful insight was my prompt engineering quality actually started declining but I was able to see more bulls**t in the AI's answers than before? I also kinda went into a productivity disease, where I began to think that there's no life without working 24/7.

And then I realized — in the modern age, you cannot really avoid AI, just need to learn how to utilize it better. So I noted down my set of insights in a document as best practices to be followed when using Generative AI. I eventually went back to using GenAI on Sep 23, 2025 12:00 PM GMT+2, with applying the above insights. But I would say the experiment was worth it.

If you think this experiment is interesting, try to withdraw from Conversational Generative AI for a specific amount of time that you're capable of. What happens in your experience, might, and in fact *will*, be different from mine. Good Luck!

And by the way, here are the 10 insights I gathered:

> **Be a meticulous researcher:** Start using Google Search meticulously. Avoid the AI responses when searching google. Do not just take the first answer that comes directly, but search for the most reliable source of the answer as well. Make sure that you actually search for the questions only which you did not know the answer to.
>
> **Avoid confirmation bias:** The problem with most generative AI is that we can prompt it in a way that steers the conversation toward our pre-existing beliefs, and it will often validate our perspective without challenging us much. Try to be aware of that.
>
> **AI is not a knowledge creator:** Realize that AI can serve as a knowledge provider (RAG, LLM model training on large data) and knowledge organizer, and not a knowledge creator or knowledge verifier.
>
> **Differentiate Good AI from Bad AI:** Know how to differentiate the Good AI responses from the Bad AI responses. The rule of thumb is — When AI generates information based on the sources you *know* are good, it is generally a good AI response.
>
> **Know and use your sources:** The probability of AI giving made-up facts is much lower when you provide a high-quality source that *you* own (like a PDF), and ask it to work on that.
>
> **AI's accuracy is never 100%:** AI never gives out 100% correct results. But a lot of the time, whether it is work, academia, or doing some high stakes tasks, the demand would be 100%. So if you're doing a task which demands 100%, do not rely on AI.
>
> **AI's solutions are just drafts:** Any solution that AI gives out, is only a starting point and has to go through tons of human-in-the loop verification and polishing.
>
> **Never use AI for irreversible tasks:** Doing irreversible tasks with AI is almost never a good idea, unless it works on highly known and verified sources and is carefully steered through using ultra-specific prompts and special MCP servers which convert random prompts to deterministic functionality.
>
> **Reasoning should come AFTER fact-checking:** When we get a result from a task and we want to know the reasoning from AI as to why we get that result, make sure to 100% fact-check that result first. AI tends to even justify bulls**t results and facts, because it frequently assumes that your result is genuine (unless it breaks a world truth).
>
> **GenAI for writing is still flawed:** Even with the latest models, AI generates extremely generic pieces of text without any human expressiveness to it. Moreover, AI tends to constantly correct our writing (a flawed side effect of its 'Always be helpful' paradigms), so be prepared to supply it with as many keywords and phrases yourself.
