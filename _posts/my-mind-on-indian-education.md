I just saw a video where an astrophysicist Janna J. Levin, who has earned a Ph.D. in theoretical physics at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, was having discussions with five different people – a child, a teen, a college student, a grad student, and an expert, about a simple yet fascinating and potentially complicated topic – gravity. 

It was interesting in the way how she talked about gravity as a simple process by which we are stuck to the earth, to the child and teenager and then moving on to the so-called ‘rocket science’ stuff, like black holes and the concept of space-time to the grad student and the expert, who were able to comprehend it quite well. Sounds simple right? Except it is often ignored and sometimes even violated in Indian schools. What is the reason? 

In Indian schools, there is no clear step-by-step process to learning. Take an easy example. If you are a science student, you would have had the experience of learning calculus in school. How does it go on? Well, they start with something like functions and limits, and even before you get over your difficulty in being able to grasp the ideas of ‘approaching a value’ or ‘approaching infinity’, they dump a bunch of new formulas and .theorems into your brains and make your head pain so much that you lose the very purpose and joy of playing around with something as interesting and fun as calculus. Now if you go and ask a grade 12 student from, say, Chennai, his/her reply would say something like, “No wonder that’s the toughest and the most stupid topic I have ever read, that is why trigonometry (just to fill in the gap) is much better.” And by the way, this is just one topic. There are so many more topics like these where a clear-cut elevation in difficulty is not ensured when moving from one grade to the next. 

There is another problem in the Indian education system which is quite a problem in most other education systems as well. The subjects and topics are taught as "content" and not as "knowledge". Well, you may ask, "knowledge is just content transfer, right?" No. Content transfer can be done to anybody, and it is all the same. Knowledge transfer not only depends on what the sender (of knowledge) knows but how well he knows about that subject. It also depends upon the ability of the receiver to comprehend the topic. Enough work has to be done to enable the student to be comfortable with the content gained. That is knowledge transfer. 

Education is a life-saving tool against some of the biggest hindrances of life. If enough effort is put into supplying food, saving lives, and defending the country, why not put the same effort into education? Why is the quality not constantly being analyzed and improved, and why is education taken as a "degree" rather than an "experience". It is our job to revolutionize.
